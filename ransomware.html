Ransomware

How ransomware can deny access to information, how attacks can progress, and what organizations can do to reduce risk and recover.

CECS 6005 · Principles of Information Security

Ransomware is a type of malicious software that can prevent targeted users from accessing their files or systems. In many attacks, the information is encrypted and the attackers demand a payment from the victim to regain access (Baker, 2025).

Attacker → infects system → encrypts files → victim loses access → attacker demands payment

Double Extortion

Attackers first steal the organization's information and then encrypt their systems. Subsequently, they can threaten to publish the stolen information if the victim does not pay.

Two forms of pressure exist in Double Extortion:

  • Encryption → you cannot access your data
  • Data theft → they can disclose the stolen information

How Can a Ransomware Attack Begin?

There are different ways such as:

  • Phishing can be by means of a user receiving a malicious email which interacts with an attachment or link which is used to compromise the system.
  • Compromised Credentials is when an attacker obtains the credentials of an account, with the purpose of being able to use them to try to access the organization's systems.
  • Weak or Reused Passwords are weak or constantly reused passwords which can increase the risk of an account being compromised.
  • Unpatched Software can contain known vulnerabilities that have not been corrected, which can provide attackers with opportunities to compromise a system.
  • Exposed Remote Services are remote access services that have been exposed or configured in an insecure way that can represent more entry points.
  • Social Engineering is the technique in which the attacker does not always need to start by technically exploiting a computer, but rather can use social techniques to try to deceive a person to steal information or even money.

What Happens After Obtaining Access?

In some cases, a ransomware attack can result in a compromised computer being encrypted quickly. However, depending on the incident, an attacker may first attempt to obtain higher privileges, access other systems, identify important information, or locate backups.

Afterward, Data Exfiltration can occur, which means taking information out of the organization without authorization.

Finally, the ransomware can be deployed and information encrypted.

Initial Access → Privilege Escalation → Lateral Movement → Data Exfiltration → Encryption → Ransom Demand

How Can an Organization Reduce the Risk?

Instead of relying on only one protection, an organization can implement different security controls such as:

  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) which adds additional protection to accounts.
  • Least Privilege which limits user permissions.
  • Patching which corrects known or discovered vulnerabilities.
  • Backups which allow having copies of important information for recovery.
  • Network Segmentation which helps separate parts of the network and limit movement between systems.
  • Monitoring which allows looking for suspicious behaviors.
  • Endpoint Protection can help detect or block certain threats on computers and other endpoints.
  • Employee Training can help employees recognize phishing and other attempts of Social Engineering.

What Happens if an Attack Occurs?

An organization has as a priority to organize itself to be able to identify which systems are affected, isolate the systems that could be compromised to limit the propagation, investigate what occurred, protect evidence when possible, identify compromised accounts, and begin the recovery process. Backups are highly important for recovery, but they must remain properly protected to prevent the attacker from also being able to compromise them.
